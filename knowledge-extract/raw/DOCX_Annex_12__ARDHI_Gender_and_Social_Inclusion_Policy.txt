About ARDHI
We are a Ugandan based NGO that drives community resilience through advocacy and systems innovations in the areas of agriculture and food security, waste management, disaster preparedness, and land governance.
Vision
A future where research informs action, and action delivers justice.
Mission
Impacting communities through research, innovations and advocacy particularly in areas of smart agriculture, waste management, disaster preparedness, health and land governance.
Our Values
Sustainability: Champion policies and actions that safeguard natural resources.
Equity: Foster inclusive environments and advocate for fair access to resources, ensuring marginalized voices are represented and empowered.
Innovation: Pioneer creative solutions and leverage emerging technologies to address complex systemic challenges.
Collaboration: Forge purposeful partnerships to amplify collective impact and drive lasting change.
Our Theory of Change
ARDHI is not a Think Tank, but a Think and Act Tank. Established in 2024 and fully registered in 2025, we operate on the belief that if we combine advocacy to shift rules, research to generate empirical evidence, and innovation to pilot practical models across agriculture, waste, disaster preparedness, health, and land governance, communities can gain the tools, voice, and systems they need to solve persistent problems. Lasting impact requires more than just ideas, it requires pressure, proof, and prototypes working together.
Our offices are located at Plot 1207, Ggaba Road, Next to the American Embassy, Kampala, Uganda. For general enquiries, contact us at info@ardhi.org.ug or visit www.ardhi.org.ug.

1. Introduction
Land is the foundation of livelihood, identity and security in Uganda, yet access to it is not shared equally. Women hold and use most agricultural land but own only a small share of it. Widows and separated women are evicted by relatives. Young people inherit late or not at all. Persons with disabilities are left out of meetings held in places they cannot reach and in formats they cannot follow. Pastoralists, fisherfolk, forest dependent communities, refugees and host communities face rules that were written without them. Ethnic minorities and people living in informal settlements are often invisible in land records altogether.
ARDHI Law and Policy Initiative exists to change the status quo through advocacy, research and innovation. A land justice organisation that does not deliberately include the people who are most often excluded will simply repeat the pattern it is trying to break. This Gender and Social Inclusion Policy sets out how ARDHI makes inclusion a working practice rather than a statement of intent.
Inclusion is not an add on to our programmes. It is the test of whether our programmes worked at all.
1.1 Purpose
To set out ARDHI commitments on gender equality, disability inclusion and social inclusion in both our programmes and our workplace.
To give staff practical standards and tools they can apply to any activity.
To establish minimum requirements that every ARDHI project, proposal and partner must meet.
To define how ARDHI measures, reports on and learns from inclusion performance.
1.2 Scope
This policy applies to all ARDHI staff, board members, volunteers, interns, consultants, partners, sub grantees and suppliers, and to every project, research study, advocacy campaign, publication, event and recruitment process carried out in the name of ARDHI.

2. Definitions

Term
 | Meaning used in this policy
 | 
Gender
 | The social roles, behaviours and expectations attached to being a woman, a man, a girl or a boy in a given society. Gender roles are learned and can change.
 | 
Gender equality
 | Women, men, girls and boys enjoy the same rights, resources, opportunities and protection. It does not mean treating everyone the same, because equal treatment of unequal starting points produces unequal results.
 | 
Gender equity
 | The fair treatment needed to reach equality, which may require different or additional measures for those who start from a disadvantage.
 | 
Social inclusion
 | Removing the barriers of attitude, environment and institution that stop any group from taking part in and benefiting from a programme.
 | 
Intersectionality
 | The way several characteristics combine to deepen exclusion, for example an older widow with a physical disability living on customary land.
 | 
Reasonable accommodation
 | The practical adjustment that allows a person with a disability to take part on an equal basis, such as a sign language interpreter, a ground floor venue or materials in large print.
 | 
Gender mainstreaming
 | Making the concerns of women and men an integral part of design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation, so that all groups benefit and inequality is not repeated.
 | 
Transformative approach
 | Going beyond counting women in a room, and working on the norms, laws and power relations that keep exclusion in place.
 | 
GESI
 | Gender Equality and Social Inclusion, used as a single working term in this policy.
 | 

3. Guiding Principles
Equality of rights and of results
ARDHI measures success by what different groups actually obtain, not by how many were invited. A training where thirty women attended but no woman spoke has not succeeded.
Deliberate targeting
Where a group faces a specific barrier, ARDHI designs a specific response rather than assuming that a general activity will reach them.
Nothing about them without them
Women, young people, persons with disabilities and other excluded groups take part in designing, delivering and reviewing the activities that concern them. ARDHI works with and through organisations led by these groups, including women land rights networks and organisations of persons with disabilities.
Do no harm
Work on gender can provoke backlash inside households and communities. Every activity is screened for the risk it may create for participants, and men and traditional leaders are engaged as allies rather than treated as opponents.
Respect and dignity
Language, images and examples used by ARDHI never present any group as helpless, and never use disability, poverty or widowhood as a spectacle.
Accountability
Inclusion targets sit in workplans, budgets and performance appraisals. What is not measured and funded does not happen.

 | 4.2 Gender responsive land and legal programming
Support women to be named on certificates of customary ownership, on communal land association membership lists and on titles, jointly with a spouse where the household agrees, and in their own right where the land is theirs.
Insist on documented spousal consent in all land transactions supported by ARDHI, and explain the legal effect of consent to both spouses separately.
Provide legal aid and mediation on widow and orphan dispossession, boundary disputes, forced eviction and land grabbing, with a target that at least 60 percent of legal aid clients are women.
Work with clan leaders, area land committees and local council courts on customary rules that discriminate, and record agreed reforms in writing.
Build the capacity of women to sit on area land committees, land boards, communal land association executives and clan structures, and follow up whether they are able to speak and vote.
Address unpaid care work as a barrier to participation by holding activities at times chosen by women and by providing child friendly arrangements where needed.
4.3 Disability inclusion
Use the Washington Group short set of questions to identify participants with disabilities rather than asking a single yes or no question.
Budget for sign language interpretation, guides, accessible transport, large print, audio versions and simplified summaries.
Check every venue against the accessibility checklist in Appendix C before it is booked.
Partner with organisations of persons with disabilities in design and delivery, and pay them properly for that role.
Ensure the ARDHI website, digital tools and mobile data collection forms follow accessible design principles.
4.4 Youth inclusion
Recognise that young people are affected by delayed inheritance, by exclusion from clan decision making and by insecure rental and informal settlement tenure.
Create paid internship and paralegal positions for young women and men in every project of twelve months or more.
Use radio, community drama, short video and social media alongside meetings, because these reach young people better.

5. Workplace Commitments
5.1 Recruitment and staffing
Job adverts state that ARDHI is an equal opportunity employer and that women, young people and persons with disabilities are encouraged to apply.
Shortlists for every position include qualified women wherever they applied. Where a shortlist has no woman, the hiring manager records the reason in writing.
Interview panels have at least one woman and use the same scored questions for all candidates.
Where two candidates score equally, preference goes to the candidate from the group that is under represented in that team.
ARDHI aims for at least 50 percent women across staff, at least 40 percent women in senior management and at least 5 percent staff with disabilities.
Reasonable accommodation is offered at application, at interview and in employment.
5.2 Conditions of work
Equal pay for work of equal value, tested through an annual pay review by grade and sex.
Maternity and paternity leave at least at the level required by the Employment Act, and flexible return to work arrangements.
A private, clean space for nursing mothers at the head office.
Flexible and hybrid working where the role allows it.
Accessible offices, meeting rooms and sanitation.
Zero tolerance of sexual harassment, applied through Annex 11: Safeguarding Policy.
5.3 Voice and culture
A staff GESI committee, chaired by a member of management and with elected staff representatives, meets quarterly.
An annual anonymous staff survey covers respect, safety, fairness of promotion and experience of discrimination.
GESI performance is a scored objective in the appraisal of every manager.
Meetings are chaired so that junior staff and women are heard, and speaking time is monitored by the chair.

Every project and proposal is scored using the following marker at design, at mid term and at completion. A score of at least 2 is required for approval.

 | Description
 |

 | Blind

 | 7.3 Reporting
Quarterly programme reports include a GESI section showing disaggregated results and explaining any gap against target.
The annual report includes a GESI performance statement approved by the Board.
Donor reports follow the disaggregation required by the specific grant, and at minimum by sex, age and disability.
Learning from GESI is fed into the learning agenda described in Annex 14.

8. Complaints, Grievances and Non Compliance
Any person who believes they have been discriminated against, excluded or harassed by ARDHI or its partners may complain through the channels set out in Annex 11 and in the community feedback mechanism in Annex 13. Complaints of a safeguarding nature are handled under Annex 11. Complaints about exclusion from a programme are investigated by the GESI Focal Person and answered within fifteen working days.
Failure by a staff member, partner or supplier to apply this policy is a performance matter and, where it is deliberate or repeated, a disciplinary matter. A partner that repeatedly fails to meet the minimum standards may have its agreement suspended.

Appendix A: GESI Analysis Framework
Completed before design is finalised, using secondary data, community consultation and separate discussions with women, men, young people and persons with disabilities.

Domain
 | Questions to answer
 | Data sources
 | 
Access to resources
 | Who holds, uses and controls land, water, trees, credit, inputs and information in this area? What proportion of land documents name a woman?
 | Land office records, household survey, focus groups
 | 
Decision making and voice
 | Who sits on area land committees, clan structures and local councils? Who speaks in meetings and whose views carry weight?
 | Attendance records, observation, key informant interviews
 | 
Roles and time use
 | How is work and unpaid care divided? What times of day and year are people available?
 | Daily and seasonal calendars
 | 
Laws, rules and norms
 | What do statute, custom and religion say about inheritance and about the land rights of women, youth and persons with disabilities? Where do they conflict?
 | Legal review, clan leader interviews
 | 
Barriers to participation
 | What stops women, young people or persons with disabilities from attending, speaking or benefiting? Distance, cost, language, permission, accessibility, safety?
 | Focus groups, accessibility audit
 | 
Risks and backlash
 | What harm could this project cause? Could working with women provoke violence or exclusion at home?
 | Focus groups, safeguarding risk assessment
 | 
Capacities and allies
 | Which women groups, youth groups, disability organisations, male champions and leaders can we work with?
 | Stakeholder mapping

Washington Group short set questions, asked as: Do you have difficulty seeing, even if wearing glasses? Do you have difficulty hearing, even if using a hearing aid? Do you have difficulty walking or climbing steps? Do you have difficulty remembering or concentrating? Do you have difficulty with self care such as washing all over or dressing?
