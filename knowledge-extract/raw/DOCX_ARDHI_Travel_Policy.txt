1.  Purpose
This Travel Policy sets out how ARDHI Law & Policy Initiative (“ARDHI”) authorises, arranges, funds and accounts for official travel. It aims to ensure that travel is necessary, safe, economical and properly documented — protecting both the traveller’s wellbeing and the funds (often donor funds) that pay for the journey.
2.  Scope
This policy covers all domestic and international travel undertaken on ARDHI business — field visits, community activities, meetings, training, conferences and disaster-response deployments — by any person travelling for or on behalf of ARDHI. It operates together with the Cash Management Procedures (advances and accountability), the Delegation of Authority (approvals) and the Fixed Asset Policy (use of vehicles).

 | 4.  Principles
Necessity. Travel is undertaken only where it is essential to ARDHI’s work and cannot reasonably be replaced by a call or virtual meeting.
Economy and value for money. The most cost-effective safe option is chosen; trips are combined where possible.
Safety first. The safety of travellers is never compromised for cost, particularly in field and high-risk areas.
Accountability. All travel is authorised in advance and fully accounted for with receipts.
No personal gain. Travel funds meet actual, reasonable costs — they are not a source of personal profit.

No official travel or travel expense is incurred without prior written authorisation by the officer empowered under the Delegation of Authority.

6.  Travel Planning and Booking
Travel is planned early to secure better fares and rates; last-minute booking is avoided except for emergencies.
Air travel is by economy class on the most direct reasonable routing; upgrades are at the traveller’s own cost.
Where several staff attend the same event, attendance is limited to those whose presence is necessary.
7.  Modes of Transport
ARDHI or hired vehicle. Used for field and group travel; driven only by authorised, licensed drivers, with a journey log.
Public / scheduled transport. Buses and reputable operators are used for economy where safe.
Boda-boda (motorcycle taxi). Used only for short, necessary trips, with a helmet, at reasonable speed, and avoided at night or in unsafe conditions.
Personal vehicle. Where authorised, reimbursed at an approved mileage rate; the owner is responsible for the vehicle’s roadworthiness and insurance.
8.  Per Diem / Daily Subsistence Allowance
Per diem covers meals and incidental costs when a traveller is away from the duty station overnight. Rates are set by the Board and reviewed periodically; they are defaults to be confirmed.

9.  Accommodation
Accommodation is safe, clean, reasonably priced and appropriate to the location — not luxury.
Where ARDHI books and pays accommodation directly, per diem is reduced accordingly to avoid double payment.
10.  Travel Advances and Accountability
A travel advance may be issued for authorised costs that cannot practically be prepaid, following the Cash Management Procedures.
Accountability: a travel advance is accounted for within seven (7) working days of return, with receipts; any unspent balance is returned and banked.
A further advance is not issued until a previous advance is fully accounted for.
11.  Expense Accountability and Receipts
Claims are made on a Travel Expense/Accountability Report (Annex B) with original receipts for transport, accommodation and other reimbursable costs.
Per diem does not require meal receipts but is paid only for nights actually spent away, evidenced by the trip.
A brief trip report (Annex C) is submitted for substantive field visits, training and external meetings.
12.  International Travel
International travel requires ED approval and is planned to allow for visas, passports and required vaccinations.
Travellers carry adequate travel and medical insurance; the cost is an eligible travel expense.
Travellers receive a briefing on the destination, the purpose, security and reporting expectations before departure.
13.  Field and High-Risk Travel Safety
ARDHI’s work includes disaster-affected and field locations. For such travel:
A risk assessment is done before deployment; travel to unsafe areas is avoided or postponed.
Travellers share an itinerary and check in with a designated contact at agreed times; a no-contact protocol is followed if check-in is missed.
Travellers avoid night driving on unsafe roads, carry a charged phone and emergency contacts, and follow local security advice.
Lone travel to high-risk areas is avoided; travellers move in pairs or teams where feasible.
14.  Duty of Care and Safeguarding
ARDHI owes a duty of care to travellers; travellers in turn follow safety instructions and act responsibly.
While travelling and in communities, all persons uphold ARDHI’s safeguarding standards and code of conduct; any safeguarding concern is reported under the Whistleblowing Policy.
15.  Use of ARDHI Vehicles
ARDHI vehicles are driven only by authorised drivers holding a valid licence, for official purposes.
A vehicle log records every journey, driver, distance, purpose and fuel; fuel is reconciled to journeys.
Private use of ARDHI vehicles is not permitted without written authorisation; traffic laws are obeyed and drivers are personally responsible for fines arising from their own violations.
16.  Prohibited Practices
Claiming per diem or costs for travel not undertaken, or for nights not spent away;
Double-claiming (e.g. per diem plus paid meals), or inflating receipts;
Booking a more expensive option for personal benefit;
Undertaking unauthorised travel and seeking reimbursement afterwards.
Such acts are treated as misconduct and, where dishonest, under the Fraud Policy.
17.  Environmental Consideration
Consistent with ARDHI’s mission, travel is minimised through virtual meetings where effective, trips are combined, and shared transport is used where practical.
