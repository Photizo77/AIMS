About ARDHI
We are a Ugandan based NGO that drives community resilience through advocacy and systems innovations in the areas of agriculture and food security, waste management, disaster preparedness, and land governance.
Vision
A future where research informs action, and action delivers justice.
Mission
Impacting communities through research, innovations and advocacy particularly in areas of smart agriculture, waste management, disaster preparedness, health and land governance.
Our Values
Sustainability: Champion policies and actions that safeguard natural resources.
Equity: Foster inclusive environments and advocate for fair access to resources, ensuring marginalized voices are represented and empowered.
Innovation: Pioneer creative solutions and leverage emerging technologies to address complex systemic challenges.
Collaboration: Forge purposeful partnerships to amplify collective impact and drive lasting change.
Our Theory of Change
ARDHI is not a Think Tank, but a Think and Act Tank. Established in 2024 and fully registered in 2025, we operate on the belief that if we combine advocacy to shift rules, research to generate empirical evidence, and innovation to pilot practical models across agriculture, waste, disaster preparedness, health, and land governance, communities can gain the tools, voice, and systems they need to solve persistent problems. Lasting impact requires more than just ideas, it requires pressure, proof, and prototypes working together.
Our offices are located at Plot 1207, Ggaba Road, Next to the American Embassy, Kampala, Uganda. For general enquiries, contact us at info@ardhi.org.ug or visit www.ardhi.org.ug.

1. Introduction and Purpose
ARDHI Law and Policy Initiative works on land, natural resources and policy. Even work that is mainly legal, advisory and advocacy in nature can create environmental and social effects. Community mapping can trigger disputes over boundaries. Support to land documentation can leave out those with weaker claims. Small works such as a borehole, an office renovation, a demonstration woodlot or a community resource centre disturb land and water. Convening people creates safety and health risks. This document sets out how ARDHI identifies, avoids, reduces and manages those effects.
This Environmental and Social Safeguards framework is written so that it can be attached directly to a donor application, and so that a project team can use it on the ground without further translation.
1.1 Objectives
To ensure that no ARDHI activity causes avoidable harm to people, to property or to the environment.
To screen every activity before it begins and to classify its level of risk.
To set out the management measures, responsibilities, budget and monitoring that follow from each risk level.
To provide an accessible grievance redress mechanism for anyone affected by ARDHI work.
To meet Ugandan environmental and social law and the safeguard requirements of our funding partners.
1.2 Scope
This framework applies to every ARDHI project, research activity, campaign, event, construction or rehabilitation work, procurement and partner activity. It applies from the point at which an activity is designed until it is completed and closed.

2. Guiding Principles
Avoid first, then reduce, then restore, then compensate
ARDHI applies the mitigation hierarchy in that order. An impact that can be avoided by changing the site, the method or the timing is avoided rather than managed.
Precaution
Where an effect is uncertain but could be serious, ARDHI acts as if it will occur.
Free, prior and informed consent and meaningful consultation
People whose land, resources or livelihoods may be affected are consulted early, in their own language, with enough information and enough time, and their agreement is recorded. No ARDHI supported activity proceeds on land where the occupants have not agreed.
No involuntary resettlement
ARDHI does not carry out or support any activity that requires people to move against their will or that restricts their access to resources without their agreement and fair compensation.
Inclusion and non discrimination
Consultation reaches women, young people, persons with disabilities, older persons, tenants and occupants without documents, not only landowners and leaders. This is applied together with Annex 12.
Transparency and accountability
Screening results, management plans and grievance records are open to the affected community and to the funder.
Continuous improvement
Findings from monitoring and from grievances are used to change practice, and the change is recorded.

3. Environmental and Social Screening
Every activity is screened before approval, using the form in Appendix A. Screening is completed by the Project Manager together with the Environmental and Social Safeguards Focal Person, and is approved by the Country Director.

 | 3.2 Exclusion list
ARDHI will not fund, implement or support any activity that:
Requires involuntary physical or economic displacement of any person.
Involves the conversion or degradation of a wetland, a central or local forest reserve, a national park, a wildlife reserve or a critical natural habitat.
Uses child labour, forced labour or unsafe labour practices, whether directly or through a contractor.
Involves the purchase or use of pesticides classified as extremely or highly hazardous by the World Health Organization.
Damages or removes cultural, spiritual or archaeological property.
Takes place on contested land where the dispute is before a court, unless the activity is expressly a dispute resolution activity agreed by all parties.
Would legitimise a claim to land at the expense of a legitimate tenure right holder who has not been consulted.
Proceeds without the recorded consent of the lawful and bona fide occupants of the land concerned.

 | Anonymised reporting, safety planning, referral to legal aid and to protection actors

Risk
 | How it could arise
 | Management measure
 | 
Soil disturbance and erosion
 | Excavation for a borehole, latrine or foundation
 | Limit the working area; work in the dry season; restore and replant on completion; silt traps on slopes

Vegetation and tree loss
 | Site clearance for small works

 | Construction rubble, packaging, electronic waste, printed materials

 | 
Air quality, dust and noise
 | Works near homes, schools or health facilities
 | Damp down dust; restrict works to daytime hours; notify neighbours in advance
 | 
Climate and disaster risk
 | Siting a structure in a flood prone or landslide prone area
 | Screen the site against flood and landslide risk; raise plinths; drainage; avoid steep slopes
 | 
Carbon and resource use
 | Travel, printing, energy use
 | Combine field trips; use online meetings where they do not exclude anyone; solar power at the resource centre; print double sided and reduce printing
 | 
Biodiversity
 | Works near a wetland or forest edge
 | Buffer distances observed; no activity inside a protected area without written authority

5. Environmental and Social Management Plan
Where an activity is screened as Category B1 or B2, the Project Manager prepares a management plan using the template in Appendix B before the activity begins. The plan is short, practical and costed. It is approved by the Safeguards Focal Person and is attached to the activity file. No funds are released for the activity until the plan is approved.
A management plan must state, for every significant risk, the mitigation measure, who is responsible, when it will be done, how much it costs and the indicator that shows it was done. Costs are included in the project budget and are not treated as an optional extra.
5.1 Contractor and supplier requirements
Where ARDHI engages a contractor for any works, the contract includes the following as binding conditions:
Compliance with this framework and with the approved management plan.
A signed Code of Conduct for every worker, in line with Annex 11.
No child labour and no forced labour, with age verification for all workers.
Provision of personal protective equipment at the cost of the contractor, and a site safety induction.
Local recruitment for unskilled work, with a target of at least 30 percent women.
Immediate reporting of any accident, spill, chance find or community complaint.
Site restoration on completion, and removal of all waste.
The right of ARDHI to suspend payment or terminate for a safeguard breach.

7. Grievance Redress Mechanism
Anyone affected by an ARDHI activity may raise a grievance, free of charge, without any need to give a reason for their standing, and without fear of losing any benefit. The mechanism does not replace the right to go to court or to any other authority.
7.1 Channels
A grievance box at every activity venue and at each field office, opened weekly by two officers together.
A telephone and message line, with the number displayed at every venue and read out at every meeting.
Email to grievance@ardhilaw.org.
In person to any ARDHI staff member, who records it and passes it on within twenty four hours.
Through a community grievance contact, at least half of whom are women, for people who cannot read or write.
Through a local council or partner focal point where the community prefers that route.
Grievances that concern sexual exploitation, abuse or harassment are transferred immediately and confidentially to the Safeguarding Focal Person and are handled under Annex 11. They are never handled as ordinary grievances.

ARDHI budgets not less than 2 percent of direct project costs for environmental and social safeguard implementation, including consultation, grievance handling, training, monitoring and restoration. All staff receive a safeguards briefing at induction and an annual refresher. Project Managers receive additional training on screening and management planning.
9. Monitoring, Reporting and Review
Safeguard compliance is checked during every routine monitoring visit using the checklist in Appendix E.
The Safeguards Focal Person prepares a quarterly safeguard report covering screening decisions, management plan implementation, incidents, grievances and corrective actions.
Any serious incident, meaning a fatality, a serious injury, a significant environmental damage, an eviction or a safeguarding allegation, is reported to the Country Director immediately and to the donor within forty eight hours.
An annual safeguard performance review is presented to the Board and informs the update of the risk register in Annex 15.
This framework is reviewed every two years or when the law or donor requirements change.

Appendix D: Chance Find Procedure
A chance find is any object, structure or remain of possible archaeological, historical, cultural or spiritual value that is discovered unexpectedly during works. Where a chance find occurs:
Stop all work in the immediate area at once.
Do not touch, move, photograph for public use, or allow anyone to remove the find.
Secure and mark the area, and place a guard where necessary.
Notify the ARDHI Project Manager within two hours, and the Safeguards Focal Person the same day.
The Project Manager notifies the local council, the district cultural or community development officer and, where the find appears significant, the Department of Museums and Monuments.
Consult the elders or custodians of the site on what is appropriate.
Work resumes only after written clearance from the responsible authority, and the area may be excluded from the works permanently.
Record the find, the notifications and the outcome in the safeguard register.
