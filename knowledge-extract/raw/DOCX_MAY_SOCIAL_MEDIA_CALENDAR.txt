MAY SOCISL MEDIA CALENDAR
Here are 8 distinct visual directions to share with your graphic designer.

The Core Design Principle: "System vs Reality"
Before diving into the 8 directions, establish this core rule: Every piece of content ARDHI creates should visually represent the tension between a theoretical system (neat, organized, policy) and the reality on the ground (messy, human, complex).

This distinction can be shown through:

Typography: (e.g., Sans Serif for Policy vs. Hand-drawn for Reality)

Color: (e.g., Brand Blue for Policy vs. Brand Orange/Green for Reality)

Texture: (e.g., Clean lines vs. Grain/Texture)

Direction 1: The "Split Screen" (Dual-Concept Layout)
Best Used For: Myth vs Reality (LinkedIn), Field vs Policy (Instagram Carousel), or any post requiring direct comparison.

Visual Description: The canvas is divided cleanly down the middle (horizontally or vertically).

Side A (e.g., Left): Represents the theoretical, organized "System" or "Policy." This side uses clean typography, ample white space, and maybe an abstract, stylized icon. Use ARDHI's primary brand color.

Side B (e.g., Right): Represents the complex "Reality" or "Fact." This side must feature a high-quality, authentic photo that grounds the concept in real life (e.g., a farmer, a landfill, a flooded street). Use a secondary, contrasting brand color for accents or text overlays.

Graphic Elements:

A centered, slightly broken or dashed dividing line.

Clean headings (#MYTH/#REALITY)

VISUAL DIRECTION
Direction 1: The "Split Screen" (Dual-Concept Layout)
Best Used For: Myth vs Reality (LinkedIn), Field vs Policy (Instagram Carousel), or any post requiring direct comparison.
Visual Description: The canvas is divided cleanly down the middle (horizontally or vertically).
Side A (e.g., Left): Represents the theoretical, organized "System" or "Policy." This side uses clean typography, ample white space, and maybe an abstract, stylized icon. Use ARDHI's primary brand color.
Side B (e.g., Right): Represents the complex "Reality" or "Fact." This side must feature a high-quality, authentic photo that grounds the concept in real life (e.g., a farmer, a landfill, a flooded street). Use a secondary, contrasting brand color for accents or text overlays.
Graphic Elements:
A centered, slightly broken or dashed dividing line.
Clean headings (#MYTH/#REALITY)
Designer Note: Ensure the photo side isn't just a "stock" photo. It needs texture and context. The goal is tension.
Direction 2: The "Layered Blueprint" (Process Illustration)
Best Used For: Inside the Work (LinkedIn), explaining complex processes (Reels/Graphics).
Visual Description: This direction makes the invisible systems visible. Think of an architectural blueprint or a technical schematic. Use a dark background (like a traditional blueprint or ARDHI's darkest brand color) and thin, technical, line-art style graphics.
Graphic Elements:
Flowcharts, diagrams, arrows, and connectivity icons (e.g., gear, lightbulb, data points).
Key text (the main message) is rendered in a light, "blueprint white" or "blueprint blue."
Small, realistic details might "burst" through the blueprint layer—like a small, photo-realistic image of a truck stuck in a storage node, illustrating a breakdown in the flow.
Designer Note: Keep it legible. It should feel smart and technical, but not overly busy.
Direction 3: The "Map Overlay" (Spatial Context)
Best Used For: Post-harvest lossMapping, Disaster preparedness mapping, Land governance.
Visual Description: This style uses a graphic representation of a map (topographic, city grid, or land parcel map) as the primary background or base layer. This map should be subtle (e.g., low-opacity gray or one brand color).
Graphic Elements:
ARDHI’s data is "plotted" onto this map.
Use vibrant, contrasting colors (e.g., a "heat map" effect of orange to show high-loss areas).
Employ icons that relate directly to the content (e.g., a tiny truck icon moving along a route, with loss-points marked).
Add a stylized compass rose or coordinate grid to reinforce the idea of "mapping the problem."
Designer Note: This turns abstract concepts into real-world geography. The map should be simplified, not a detailed cartographic map.
Direction 4: The "Hand-Annotated Report" (Human Insight)
Best Used For: Co-Creation, Inside the Work (any content emphasizing lived experience).
Visual Description: This direction makes the graphics feel active and in-progress, as if you’re looking at ARDHI’s field notes or a working document.
Graphic Elements:
A base layer that looks like a clean digital report or official document (e.g., with a grid line or subtle notebook texture).
The official text (e.g., "Field Observations") is in standard, clean typography.
The critical insights, key data, or the community feedback are added as "hand-written" (script font or hand-drawn font) annotations, circles, arrows, and underlined text. Use a different, brighter color for the handwriting (like red or orange ink) to draw the eye.
Designer Note: This style screams: "We are listening to the ground." The contrast between the formal text and the informal annotations is the key.
Direction 5: The "Bold Typographic Statement" (Core Message)
Best Used For: X (Twitter) Threads (intro graphics), Engagement Graphics, Poll Intros.
Visual Description: When the message is the most important element, typography should dominate. This style dispenses with images to let the core challenge stand on its own.
Graphic Elements:
Massive, heavy, bold typography. Choose one brand typeface for maximum impact.
The central question or statement (e.g., "SYSTEMS FAIL") fills 60-70% of the graphic.
Use high-contrast color combinations (e.g., bright orange text on a deep navy background).
Text can be slightly distorted or broken to convey the concept (e.g., the word "FRAGMENTED" might have gaps in the letters).
Designer Note: Think "billboard" design. It must be readable from a distance and make an immediate impact.
Direction 6: The "Macro-Textured Close-up" (Impact)
Best Used For: Micro-stories (X), Disaster preparedness (evoking emotion), Land (texture).
Visual Description: This style focuses on the emotional or material impact of a system failure. It uses extreme, high-detail macro photography of objects or textures associated with the theme.
Graphic Elements:
The main image is an ultra-close-up with intense texture and shallow depth of field (e.g., the grain in a piece of spilled, unusable grain; the rusted metal of a broken irrigation valve; the dry cracks in an unmanaged plot of land).
The color palette is natural, earthy, and gritty.
The text (one simple, powerful statement or story) is small, minimal, and perhaps slightly transparent or positioned to the side, forcing the viewer to confront the image first.
Designer Note: This style builds empathy and immediate connection to the real-world problem.
Direction 7: The "Interactive Interface" (Digital Co-Creation)
Best Used For: Co-Creation/Engagement, Polls (X), "Food system check-in" (Instagram).
Visual Description: This style makes the user feel like they are interacting with a digital tool or questionnaire from ARDHI.
Graphic Elements:
Design the graphic to look like a clean, simple digital interface (e.g., a phone screen or tablet app).
The content is presented as questions, checkboxes, dropdown lists, or rating scales (e.g., a "Food System Health Score" graphic).
Use icons and UI elements like toggles, sliders, and submit buttons.
The color palette should be crisp and clean—bright blues and whites with clear call-to-action colors (e.g., a green "Submit" button).
Designer Note: This is all about engagement. It gamifies the complex information and invites participation.
Direction 8: The "ARDHI Data Visualization" (Insights)
Best Used For: Explaining Reel content (static fallback), X (Twitter) threads (second post in thread).
Visual Description: Data visualization is ARDHI’s language of authority. This direction uses clean, standard data visualization practices but gives them an ARDHI signature.
Graphic Elements:
Clean charts (bar, line, or pie).
Crucial: Always link the data to the "Human Cost." The Y-axis might measure "Storage Volume (Tons)," but the X-axis should be linked to "Weeks After Harvest" and include a text label like "Farmer Revenue Loss Point."
Use ARDHI's full brand color palette to differentiate categories (e.g., Waste Collection vs. Waste Disposal).
Include a clear title and a 1-sentence "key takeaway."
Designer Note: This style should be authoritative, fact-based, and easy to read quickly. It turns abstract concepts into compelling evidence.
