1.  Purpose
This Training & Development Policy sets out how ARDHI Law & Policy Initiative (“ARDHI”) builds the skills and knowledge of its people so they can deliver ARDHI’s mission across advocacy, research and innovation. Investing in staff learning strengthens performance, supports retention and helps ARDHI adapt and grow. It expands on Section 34 of the ARDHI HR Manual.
2.  Scope
This policy applies to all employees. Relevant parts also apply to volunteers and interns. It operates together with the HR Manual, the Performance Management Policy (which identifies development needs) and the Leave Policy (study leave).
3.  Principles
Relevance to mission. Training supports ARDHI’s work and the individual’s role.
Equity of access. Development opportunities are allocated fairly and transparently, not on favouritism.
Value for money. The most cost-effective, quality option is chosen within available resources.
Transfer of learning. Staff apply what they learn and share it with colleagues.
Continuous learning. Learning is ongoing, not a one-off event.
4.  Objectives
Close skills gaps identified through performance management and organisational needs;
Equip staff to perform current roles well and prepare for future ones;
Ensure all staff complete mandatory training (Section 12);
Build a culture of learning, knowledge-sharing and improvement.

 | 6.  Identifying Training Needs
Development needs are identified mainly through the performance management cycle (appraisals and PIPs);
Organisational needs are identified from ARDHI’s plans, new projects, donor requirements and changes in law or technology;
HR consolidates needs into an annual Training Needs Assessment.
7.  Annual Training Plan and Budget
Each year HR prepares an Annual Training Plan and indicative budget, approved by the Executive Director, prioritising mandatory training and the highest-value needs;
Where funds are limited, opportunities are prioritised using the criteria in Section 9, ensuring fair access over time;
Donor-funded training is delivered in line with the relevant grant.
8.  Nomination and Approval
Training is requested on a Training Request/Nomination Form (Annex A) and approved within the Delegation of Authority (individual training by the line manager/ED depending on cost; significant sponsorship by the ED, and major cost by the Board);
Approval considers relevance, cost, provider quality, timing and operational cover.
9.  Prioritisation Criteria
Where demand exceeds available funds, training is prioritised by:
Whether it is mandatory or legally/donor-required;
The strength of the link to current role performance and organisational priorities;
The number of staff who will benefit (e.g. cascade/in-house options);
Value for money and equity of access across the team.
10.  Sponsorship and Cost-Sharing
ARDHI may fully or partly fund training; the level of support is stated at approval;
For significant external courses, ARDHI may share costs with the employee or require the employee to meet incidental costs.
11.  Service Commitment (Bonding) for Sponsored Training

Protecting ARDHI’s investment. Where ARDHI funds substantial training (for example a professional qualification), the employee signs a training agreement (Annex B) committing to remain in ARDHI’s service for a defined minimum period afterwards, or to refund a proportion of the cost if they leave early. The bonding period and terms are proportionate to the cost.

12.  Mandatory Training
Regardless of role, all staff (and, as relevant, volunteers) complete ARDHI’s mandatory training, which includes:
Safeguarding and Protection from Sexual Exploitation, Abuse and Harassment (PSEAH);
The Staff Code of Conduct;
Anti-fraud, anti-corruption and whistleblowing;
Data protection and confidentiality; and
Health, safety and (for field staff) security.
13.  Study Leave
Time off for approved, ARDHI-supported study or examinations is granted in line with the Leave Policy, balancing the employee’s development with operational needs.
14.  Application of Learning and Knowledge-Sharing
Staff who attend training apply the learning in their work and share key insights with colleagues (e.g. a short debrief or session);
A brief post-training report (Annex C) captures what was learned and how it will be used.
15.  Evaluation of Training
ARDHI evaluates whether training delivered value — through participant feedback, application on the job, and its effect on performance — so that future training decisions are better informed and value for money improves over time.
16.  Records
HR maintains a training record for each employee and an organisational training log, retained as part of personnel records under the Data Protection and Privacy Act, 2019.
