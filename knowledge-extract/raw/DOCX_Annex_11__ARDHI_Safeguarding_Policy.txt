About We Are
We are a Ugandan based NGO that drives community resilience through advocacy and systems innovations in the areas of agriculture and food security, waste management, disaster preparedness, and land governance.
Vision
A future where research informs action, and action delivers justice.
Mission
Impacting communities through research, innovations and advocacy particularly in areas of smart agriculture, waste management, disaster preparedness, health and land governance.
Our Values
Sustainability: Champion policies and actions that safeguard natural resources.
Equity: Foster inclusive environments and advocate for fair access to resources, ensuring marginalized voices are represented and empowered.
Innovation: Pioneer creative solutions and leverage emerging technologies to address complex systemic challenges.
Collaboration: Forge purposeful partnerships to amplify collective impact and drive lasting change.
Our Theory of Change
ARDHI is not a Think Tank, but a Think and Act Tank. Established in 2024 and fully registered in 2025, we operate on the belief that if we combine advocacy to shift rules, research to generate empirical evidence, and innovation to pilot practical models across agriculture, waste, disaster preparedness, health, and land governance, communities can gain the tools, voice, and systems they need to solve persistent problems. Lasting impact requires more than just ideas, it requires pressure, proof, and prototypes working together.
1. Purpose and Commitment
ARDHI Law and Policy Initiative works with communities whose land, housing and natural resource rights are insecure. Many of the people we serve are poor, are not literate in English, live far from formal justice institutions and depend on our staff for information and support. That imbalance of power creates a duty of care. This policy sets out how ARDHI protects every person who comes into contact with our work from harm caused by our own staff, partners and representatives.
ARDHI has zero tolerance for sexual exploitation, sexual abuse, sexual harassment, abuse of children, abuse of adults at risk, physical abuse, bullying, exploitation of any kind and abuse of position for personal benefit. Zero tolerance means every concern is taken seriously, every report is acted upon, and no person is protected from accountability because of their seniority, their performance or their value to a project.
No project result, funding relationship or reputational interest is more important than the safety and dignity of the people we serve.
1.1 Objectives
To prevent harm by setting clear standards of behaviour for everyone who acts for ARDHI.
To make it safe, simple and confidential for anyone to raise a concern.
To respond to every concern quickly, fairly and in a way that puts the survivor first.
To meet the safeguarding requirements of our donors, of Ugandan law and of international good practice.
To build a working culture in which safeguarding is discussed openly rather than hidden.
1.2 Scope
This policy applies at all times, in and outside working hours, on and off ARDHI premises, in person and online, to:
Board members, management, employees on any type of contract, secondees and volunteers.
Interns, students on placement, researchers and academic associates.
Consultants, enumerators, translators, community mobilisers, paralegals and community based facilitators engaged by ARDHI.
Implementing partners, sub grantees, suppliers, contractors, drivers and service providers working under an ARDHI contract or grant.
Visitors, journalists, donors and photographers who take part in ARDHI activities or visit ARDHI supported communities.
Every person listed above signs the Code of Conduct in Section 5 before they begin work. Partner organisations sign the safeguarding clause set out in Section 10 as a condition of their agreement.

3. Guiding Principles
Survivor centred approach
The wishes, safety, dignity, rights and best interests of the survivor guide every decision. Survivors are believed, are told what will happen next, are offered choices and are never pressured into a course of action they do not want. Where the survivor is a child, the best interests of the child override all other considerations, including the preferences of the family or the community.
Do no harm
Every response is checked for the risk it creates for the survivor, for witnesses and for the wider community. ARDHI does not take action that would expose a survivor to retaliation, stigma or loss of livelihood without their informed agreement, unless the law or the safety of a child requires it.
Confidentiality on a need to know basis
Information about a safeguarding case is shared only with people who must have it to keep someone safe or to carry out an investigation. Files are kept in a locked cabinet and in a password protected folder held by the Safeguarding Focal Person. Names are replaced with case codes in all reporting.
Non discrimination
Protection is offered equally regardless of sex, age, disability, ethnicity, language, religion, marital status, tenure status, income, political opinion or any other characteristic.
Transparency and accountability
ARDHI reports safeguarding incidents to its Board and to affected donors within the timelines they require, and publishes the number and type of cases in its annual report without identifying any person.
Shared responsibility
Safeguarding is not the job of one officer. Every person covered by this policy has a duty to speak up. Failing to report a concern is itself a breach of this policy.

5. Code of Conduct
Every person covered by this policy signs the following Code before beginning work with ARDHI, and again at the start of every calendar year.
5.1 I will
Treat every person I meet through ARDHI work with respect, courtesy and dignity.
Be aware of the power I hold as a person who controls information, access or project benefits, and never use it for personal gain.
Follow local laws and respect local customs, except where a custom would harm a child or an adult at risk.
Plan field visits so that I am not alone with a child or an adult at risk in a private setting, and use open, visible spaces for all meetings.
Ask for informed consent before taking any photograph, video or recording, explain how it will be used, and obtain consent from a parent or guardian where the subject is a child.
Report any concern, suspicion or disclosure to the Safeguarding Focal Person within twenty four hours.
Support the survivor to reach medical, psychosocial, police or legal help if they wish it.
Keep safeguarding information confidential.
Declare any conflict of interest, including a close personal relationship with a beneficiary, a supplier or a colleague I supervise.
5.2 I will not
Engage in sexual activity with any person under the age of eighteen, regardless of the age of consent or of marriage in the local setting. Mistaken belief about the age of a child is never a defence.
Exchange money, employment, goods, services, training places, transport, legal support or any project benefit for sex or sexual favours.
Enter into a sexual relationship with a person who is a beneficiary of an ARDHI project, because such relationships rest on unequal power and undermine our work.
Use sexual, humiliating, degrading or discriminatory language, images or gestures, including through phone messages, email or social media.
Use physical punishment, threats, intimidation, bullying or any form of harassment.
Buy or use the services of sex workers while on ARDHI duty, in ARDHI vehicles, in ARDHI accommodation or while identifiable as ARDHI personnel.
Take, keep, share or access sexual images of children in any form.
Drive an ARDHI vehicle or attend a community activity under the influence of alcohol or drugs.
Offer or accept a bribe, kickback or facilitation payment.
Post identifying details, photographs or case information about beneficiaries on personal social media.
Retaliate against, threaten or discourage any person who raises a safeguarding concern.

6. Prevention Measures
6.1 Safer recruitment
ARDHI treats recruitment as the first line of defence. For every position, and for every consultant engaged for more than ten days:
Job adverts state that ARDHI is a safeguarding employer and that all offers are subject to satisfactory checks.
Application forms require a full employment history and an explanation of any gaps.
At least one interview question tests values and safeguarding judgement, and the answer is scored.
Two references are taken up in writing, one of them from the most recent employer, and both are asked directly whether any safeguarding concern was ever raised about the candidate.
A certificate of good conduct from the Uganda Police Directorate of Interpol and International Relations is required for all posts that involve direct contact with children or with adults at risk.
The signed Code of Conduct is filed before the first day of work.
The offer letter and contract contain a safeguarding clause allowing summary dismissal for a substantiated breach.
ARDHI participates in the inter agency misconduct disclosure approach. When another organisation asks ARDHI for a safeguarding reference, ARDHI answers truthfully.

 | 6.3 Safe programme design
Every activity plan includes a short safeguarding risk note that identifies who could be harmed and what will be done to prevent it.
Community meetings, legal aid clinics and training sessions are held in accessible public venues with separate, lockable and lit sanitation for women and men.
Transport and timing of activities allow participants, especially women and persons with disabilities, to travel home in daylight.
Where a project handles land documentation or compensation, at least two officers are present at any meeting where money, documents or benefits change hands.
Beneficiary lists, consent forms and case files are stored securely and are not displayed in public.
6.4 Communication, images and data
Written informed consent is taken before any photograph, video, testimony or case study is used. Consent can be withdrawn at any time.
Images show people with dignity, fully clothed, and are not sexualised.
Names, exact villages and identifying details of survivors are never published.
Personal data is processed in line with the Data Protection and Privacy Act, 2019, and is kept only as long as needed.

7. Reporting a Concern
7.1 Who must report
Every person covered by this policy must report a safeguarding concern immediately and in any case within twenty four hours of becoming aware of it. This applies to concerns about ARDHI personnel, about partner personnel and about anyone acting for ARDHI. A person does not need proof. A reasonable suspicion is enough. Deciding for yourself that a concern is not serious enough is not permitted.

7.3 Receiving a disclosure
A person who receives a disclosure should:
Find a private place, listen calmly and do not interrupt.
Believe the person and tell them that they were right to speak up.
Ask only open questions needed to understand whether someone is in immediate danger. Do not                         investigate, do not press for detail and do not ask leading questions.   
Explain honestly that the information must be passed to the Safeguarding Focal Person and that it cannot be kept secret.
Ask what the person wants to happen and record their wishes.
Address immediate safety and health needs, including the need to reach a health facility within seventy-two hours after a sexual assault.
Write down what was said, using the words of the person, as soon as possible, and sign and date the note.
Pass the report to the Safeguarding Focal Person within twenty-four hours and keep no personal copy.

 | 8.2 Investigation standards
The investigator must be trained, must have no conflict of interest and must not be in the line management of the person concerned.
Where the allegation involves senior management, a serious criminal offence or a partner organisation, an external investigator is appointed.
The person complained about is told the substance of the allegation, is given a fair chance to respond and may be accompanied at any interview.
Survivors and witnesses are interviewed by a person of the gender they choose, with an interpreter if needed, and are never brought face to face with the person complained about.
Children are interviewed only by a person trained in child friendly interviewing, in the presence of a trusted adult who is not a suspect.
The standard of proof for internal disciplinary purposes is the balance of probabilities.
8.3 Referral to authorities
Where the concern discloses a criminal offence, ARDHI supports the survivor to report to the police and to obtain a Police Form 3 for medical examination. Where the survivor is a child, or where there is a risk to others, ARDHI reports to the police and to the district probation and social welfare officer even if consent is withheld, and explains this to the survivor beforehand. An internal investigation does not replace a criminal investigation, and ARDHI takes care not to interfere with police evidence.
8.4 Support to survivors
Medical care, including post exposure prophylaxis and emergency contraception where relevant, arranged within seventy-two hours.
Psychosocial and counselling support through ARDHI referral partners.
Legal aid and support to attend court, including transport and a chaperone.
Safety planning, relocation support in extreme cases, and continued access to project benefits.
A named contact person who keeps the survivor informed throughout.
ARDHI maintains a written referral pathway for every district of operation, listing the nearest health facility offering clinical management of rape, the police child and family protection unit, the probation and social welfare officer, legal aid providers and counselling services. The pathway is reviewed every six months.
8.5 Protection from retaliation
Any person who reports in good faith is protected. Retaliation, threats, demotion, dismissal, exclusion from project benefits or social pressure against a reporter is itself a disciplinary offence and will be treated as seriously as the original concern. A report that is later found to be unsubstantiated does not attract any sanction unless it was knowingly false or malicious.

9. Records, Monitoring and Reporting
A confidential case register is maintained by the Safeguarding Focal Person. It records the case code, date received, category, whether the survivor is a child or adult, relationship of the subject to ARDHI, actions taken, outcome and closure date. It does not travel outside the secure system.
Case files are kept for seven years after closure and are then destroyed securely, except where a longer period is required by law or by an ongoing legal process.
The Safeguarding Focal Person prepares a quarterly report to the Country Director and the Board with anonymised numbers, categories and trends, and the status of the safeguarding action plan.
The Board reviews safeguarding performance at every quarterly meeting and signs an annual assurance statement.
ARDHI reports safeguarding incidents to donors in the manner and within the timelines set in each grant agreement, and never conceals an incident.
An annual safeguarding self assessment is completed against this policy, and the resulting improvement plan is budgeted and tracked.

10. Partners, Sub Grantees, Suppliers and Consultants
ARDHI is accountable for safeguarding across the whole chain of delivery. Before any agreement is signed:
A safeguarding due diligence check is carried out, scoring the partner on policy, focal person, training,      reporting channels and case handling.
Where a partner does not yet meet the standard, a capacity strengthening plan with dates is attached to the agreement and ARDHI provides support.
The agreement contains a safeguarding clause requiring the partner to apply standards at least equal to this policy, to train its personnel, to report any incident to ARDHI within twenty four hours of becoming aware of it, to cooperate with any ARDHI or donor investigation and to allow ARDHI access to relevant records.
A serious breach or a failure to report is a ground for immediate suspension of funds and termination of the agreement.
Supplier contracts and consultant terms of reference include the Code of Conduct as a schedule.
ARDHI monitors partner safeguarding through an annual review visit and through spot checks during joint field activities.

Appendix C: One Page Community Poster (Text)
The following text is translated into the main local languages of each project area, printed with simple pictures and displayed at every activity venue.
You have the right to be safe
All help from ARDHI is free. You never have to pay money, do work or give any sexual favour to receive help, training, legal support or any benefit.
No one working for ARDHI may touch you in a sexual way, ask you for sex, insult you, threaten you or hit you.
No one working for ARDHI may have any sexual contact with a person under eighteen years.
You may refuse to be photographed or recorded.
If someone behaves badly, tell us
Call or send a message to the safeguarding line displayed at this venue.
Write to safeguarding@ardhilaw.org.
Put a note in the complaint box at this venue.
Tell the community safeguarding contact for this area.
What we promise
We will listen to you and believe you.
We will keep your information private.
We will help you get medical care, counselling and legal support if you want it.
You will not lose your place in the project because you spoke up.
