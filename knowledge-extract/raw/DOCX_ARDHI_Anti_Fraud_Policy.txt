1.  Purpose
This Anti-Fraud & Anti-Corruption Policy sets out ARDHI Law & Policy Initiative’s (“ARDHI”) firm stance against fraud, corruption and dishonesty, and the measures it takes to prevent, detect and respond to them. As an organisation entrusted with public and donor funds to serve vulnerable communities, ARDHI treats the misuse of those funds as a serious betrayal that it will not tolerate.
2.  Policy Statement

Zero tolerance. ARDHI has zero tolerance for fraud, bribery, corruption and dishonesty in any form, by anyone, at any level. Every proven case is acted upon — through recovery, discipline, and referral to the authorities where appropriate — regardless of the seniority or position of those involved.
 | 3.  Scope
This policy applies to everyone connected with ARDHI — Board members, employees, volunteers, interns, consultants, contractors, suppliers and implementing partners — and to all ARDHI activities and funds, including those provided by donors. It works together with the Whistleblowing, Conflict of Interest, Procurement, Cash Management and Bank Signatory policies.

 | A situation where a private interest could improperly influence a person’s ARDHI duties (see the COI Policy).
 | 5.  Legal Framework
This policy is anchored in the laws of Uganda and in donor requirements, including:
The Anti-Corruption Act, 2009 (and amendments);
The Penal Code Act (theft, forgery, false accounting, obtaining by false pretences);
The Anti-Money Laundering Act, 2013;
The Whistleblowers Protection Act, 2010;
The Leadership Code Act and the NGO Act, Cap 109; and
The specific anti-fraud, sanctions and reporting terms of ARDHI’s grant agreements.
6.  Approach: Prevent, Detect, Respond, Deter
Prevent. Strong internal controls, segregation of duties, vetting, a clear code of conduct, and a culture of integrity.
Detect. Reconciliations, verification, audits, oversight and accessible reporting channels.
Respond. Prompt, fair investigation; recovery of losses; discipline; and referral to authorities and donors.
Deter. Visible, consistent consequences that make clear fraud is never worthwhile.

 | 8.  Fraud Prevention Measures
Robust internal controls and segregation of duties across procurement, payments, cash and assets (per the related policies);
Recruitment vetting and reference checks; screening of suppliers and partners where donors require;
A signed staff code of conduct and mandatory anti-fraud awareness on induction and periodically;
Clear approval limits (Delegation of Authority) and dual authorisation of payments (Bank Signatory Policy);
Regular reconciliations, verifications, and internal and external audit;
An open culture in which staff feel safe to raise concerns (Whistleblowing Policy).
9.  Common Fraud Risks and Red Flags
ARDHI is alert to fraud typical of the sector, including:
Procurement fraud – fictitious or connected suppliers, inflated invoices, bid-rigging, kickbacks, split orders;
Ghost beneficiaries or inflated participant/attendance numbers;
Payroll fraud – ghost workers or inflated hours;
Per diem and travel fraud – claims for travel not undertaken, double-claiming;
Cash and mobile-money fraud – unaccounted advances, diverted disbursements, personal MoMo lines;
Misuse or diversion of restricted donor funds between projects; and
Falsified documents, altered receipts, or forged signatures.
Warning signs include missing documentation, reluctance to take leave or allow counts, lifestyle inconsistent with income, and repeated round-sum transactions just below approval thresholds.
10.  Reporting Suspected Fraud
Anyone who suspects fraud or corruption has a duty to report it promptly through the channels in the Whistleblowing Policy — to a line manager, the Executive Director, the Board Chairperson, or the confidential reporting channel. Where the concern involves the Executive Director or a Board member, it is reported directly to the Board Chairperson or, if necessary, externally. Reporting in good faith is protected; failing to report known fraud is itself a breach.
11.  Response and Investigation
On receiving a credible report, ARDHI responds fairly, promptly and confidentially:
Assessment – the Executive Director (or Board Chair where the ED is implicated) assesses the report and decides whether an investigation is warranted.
Safeguarding evidence – relevant records and systems are secured immediately to prevent tampering.
Investigation team – an impartial investigator or team (internal or external) is appointed; anyone implicated plays no part in the investigation.
Precautionary measures – where justified, an implicated staff member may be suspended (on normal terms) pending the outcome; suspension is neutral and not a sanction.
Fair process – the investigation respects confidentiality, the presumption of innocence and the right of the accused to respond.
Findings – a written report sets out facts, findings, losses and recommendations.
12.  Disciplinary and Legal Action
Proven fraud results in disciplinary action up to and including dismissal, and recovery of losses from those responsible.
Cases are referred to the Police, the Inspectorate of Government or the Director of Public Prosecutions where the law requires or the seriousness warrants.
ARDHI cooperates fully with lawful investigations and prosecutions.
13.  Donor Notification
Where fraud affects donor-funded activities, ARDHI notifies the affected donor(s) promptly in line with the grant agreement, cooperates with any donor investigation, and refunds ineligible amounts where required.
14.  Protection of Those Who Report
ARDHI protects anyone who reports a genuine suspicion in good faith from retaliation of any kind, as set out in the Whistleblowing Policy. Retaliation against a reporter is itself a serious disciplinary offence. Knowingly false or malicious allegations, however, may lead to disciplinary action.
15.  Records
Reports, investigations and outcomes are recorded and retained confidentially for at least seven (7) years, accessible only to authorised persons and to auditors, donors and authorities as required.
