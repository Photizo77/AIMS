1.  Purpose
This Salary Structure & Compensation Framework establishes how ARDHI Law & Policy Initiative (“ARDHI”) sets, structures and administers staff pay. It provides a fair, consistent and transparent basis for remuneration — rewarding roles equitably according to their responsibilities, remaining affordable and funding-linked, and complying with the law. It supports the ARDHI HR Manual.

A framework for the Board to set figures. This document defines the grading structure, pay components and rules. The specific salary amounts for each grade are set and approved by the Board of Directors and inserted into the scale in Section 7. Figures are marked for insertion so that ARDHI adopts real, affordable numbers rather than assumed ones.
 | 2.  Scope
This framework applies to all employees on ARDHI’s payroll. Consultants, interns and volunteers are remunerated (fees or stipends) under their own agreements and are not placed on the salary scale. It operates with the HR Manual, the Delegation of Authority and the Performance Management Policy.
3.  Principles
Internal equity. Jobs of similar value carry similar pay; the structure is consistent across ARDHI.
External competitiveness within affordability. Pay is set to attract and retain capable people, benchmarked to the NGO sector where possible, but always within what ARDHI can sustainably afford.
Transparency of structure, confidentiality of individual pay. The grading structure and rules are open to staff; each person’s specific salary is confidential.
Equal pay and non-discrimination. Equal pay for work of equal value; no discrimination on any protected ground.
Funding-linked and sustainable. Salaries reflect available and reasonably assured funding; ARDHI does not commit to pay it cannot sustain.

 | 5.  Governance and Authority
The Board of Directors approves the salary scale, allowance schedule and any revision.
The Executive Director implements the scale, placing staff on the correct grade and notch within it and within budget.
The ED’s own remuneration is set by the Board.
All pay decisions are made within the Delegation of Authority; no manager sets pay outside the approved scale.

8.  Job Evaluation and Placement
Each position is placed in a grade through a simple, consistent job-evaluation approach considering responsibility, complexity, supervision, decision-making and required qualifications and experience;
New or changed roles are evaluated and graded before recruitment or regrading;
Regrading of a post requires justification and Board approval and is not used to give an individual a pay rise outside the normal process.

 | 10.  Statutory Deductions
From gross pay, ARDHI deducts and remits PAYE to the Uganda Revenue Authority and the employee’s NSSF contribution (5%), and pays the employer NSSF contribution (10%) in addition to salary — a total NSSF remittance of 15% — as set out in the HR Manual.
11.  Salary on Appointment
New employees normally start at the minimum of their grade’s band;
A higher starting point (up to the mid-point) may be approved by the ED where justified by exceptional qualifications or experience, within budget; above mid-point requires Board approval;
Starting salary is stated in the employment contract.

 | 13.  Salary Progression and Increments
Movement up within a grade’s band is not automatic; it depends on ARDHI’s affordability and the employee’s performance;
Where affordable and Board-approved, a periodic increment may be awarded, informed by the annual appraisal (Performance Management Policy);
An employee at the band maximum does not progress further within that grade without promotion.
14.  Promotion and Acting
Promotion. On promotion to a higher graded post (through the recruitment process), salary is set at the minimum of the new grade, or at the point giving a meaningful increase, within the band and budget, on Board/ED approval per the DoA.
Acting. An employee formally assigned to act in a higher-graded role for a sustained period may receive an acting allowance for that period, on approval.
15.  Salary Review
The Board reviews the salary scale and allowance schedule periodically — at least annually for figures — taking into account ARDHI’s financial position and funding, the cost of living, and sector benchmarks. A review does not guarantee an increase; changes depend on affordability and are approved by the Board.
16.  Payment of Salary
Frequency & method. Salaries are paid monthly, by bank transfer or mobile money, by the [INSERT: e.g. 28th] of each month.
Payslip. Each employee receives a payslip showing gross pay, allowances, statutory and other deductions, and net pay.
Payroll controls. Payroll is prepared, verified and approved by different people (segregation of duties) and paid under the Bank Signatory Policy.
17.  Confidentiality of Pay
While the grading structure and rules are transparent, each employee’s specific salary is confidential between the employee and ARDHI. Staff are asked to respect this confidentiality.
18.  Donor-Funded Positions
Salaries for donor-funded posts are set within ARDHI’s scale and within the relevant grant budget and any donor salary caps;
Where a donor rate would exceed the scale, ARDHI manages consistency to avoid internal inequity, documenting the basis;
Staff time charged to grants is supported by proper timesheets/allocation where required.
19.  Consultants, Interns and Volunteers
Consultants are paid fees under a contract for services (Procurement Policy), based on the assignment and market rates — not the salary scale;
Interns may receive a stipend where provided; volunteers are unpaid but may be reimbursed reasonable, approved expenses.
20.  Records
Payroll and salary records are maintained confidentially and retained for at least seven (7) years, under the Data Protection and Privacy Act, 2019, and are available for internal and external audit.
21.  Review
The figures in this framework are reviewed at least annually by the Board; the policy itself is reviewed at least every two (2) years. All changes are approved by the Board.
