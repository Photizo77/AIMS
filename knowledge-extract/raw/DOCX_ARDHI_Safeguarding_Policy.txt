1.  Purpose and Policy Statement

Zero tolerance. ARDHI Law & Policy Initiative (“ARDHI”) is committed to safeguarding — protecting the people it works with, especially children and vulnerable adults, from any harm caused by ARDHI, its staff or its programmes. ARDHI has zero tolerance for abuse, exploitation, harassment or neglect of any person, and for inaction in the face of a safeguarding concern.
 | This policy sets out ARDHI’s commitment, standards and procedures to prevent harm, to recognise and report concerns, and to respond in a way that puts the safety and dignity of survivors first.
2.  Scope
This policy applies to everyone connected with ARDHI — employees, Board members, volunteers, interns, consultants, contractors, partners and visitors — in all ARDHI activities, premises, field locations and online spaces. It covers safeguarding of both children (under 18) and adults at risk, and includes protection from sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment (PSEAH).
3.  Legal and Standards Framework
This policy is anchored in Ugandan law and recognised standards, including:
The Constitution of Uganda and the Children Act, Cap 62 (as amended);
The Penal Code Act (offences against the person and children);
The Prevention of Trafficking in Persons Act, 2009;
The Prohibition of Female Genital Mutilation Act, 2010, and the Domestic Violence Act, 2010;
The Employment Act, 2006 and the Employment (Sexual Harassment) Regulations, 2012;
The Data Protection and Privacy Act, 2019; and
Donor safeguarding/PSEA standards and international good practice.

 | 5.  Guiding Principles
Do no harm. ARDHI ensures its work never exposes people to harm or exploitation.
Best interests. The welfare and best interests of the child or adult at risk come first.
Survivor-centred. Responses prioritise the safety, wishes, dignity and confidentiality of the survivor.
Zero tolerance. No abuse, exploitation or harassment is tolerated or ignored.
Non-discrimination. Everyone is protected equally, regardless of any characteristic.
Shared responsibility. Safeguarding is everyone’s duty, all the time.
6.  Forms of Abuse and Harm
Physical abuse – causing physical harm.
Emotional/psychological abuse – threats, humiliation, intimidation or control.
Sexual abuse and exploitation – any non-consensual or exploitative sexual act or arrangement, and any sexual activity with a child.
Neglect – failure to meet basic needs or protect from harm.
Exploitation – taking advantage of a person for benefit, including financial exploitation of adults and child labour or trafficking.
Online harm – harm facilitated through digital tools, data or images.
7.  Sexual Exploitation, Abuse and Harassment (SEAH) – Core Standards
The following apply to everyone connected with ARDHI, at all times:
Sexual activity with a child (anyone under 18) is prohibited, regardless of local age of consent or marriage; mistaken belief about age is no defence;
Exchanging money, employment, goods, services or assistance for sex or any sexual favour is prohibited;
Any sexually exploitative, abusive or humiliating behaviour is prohibited;
Sexual relationships between staff and beneficiaries are prohibited, as they rest on inherently unequal power and undermine ARDHI’s work;
Sexual harassment of colleagues, beneficiaries or partners is prohibited.
8.  Expected Behaviour (Safeguarding Code)
Everyone connected with ARDHI must:
Treat all people with respect and protect them from harm;
Maintain professional boundaries with beneficiaries and communities;
Report any safeguarding concern immediately;
Follow safe-programming and safe-recruitment practices.
Everyone must never:
Abuse, exploit, harass, neglect or discriminate against any person;
Engage in the SEAH-prohibited conduct in Section 7;
Act in a way, or place themselves in a situation, that could reasonably be seen as abusive or exploitative.

 | 10.  Prevention
10.1   Safe recruitment
ARDHI applies safer-recruitment checks (references, police/criminal-record clearance for roles with contact with children or vulnerable people, safeguarding self-declaration, and a safeguarding interview question) under the Recruitment Policy. Every appointee signs the safeguarding commitment before starting.
10.2   Safe programming
Safeguarding risks are assessed when designing and delivering activities, and mitigations built in;
Activities involving children or vulnerable people are designed so no adult is inappropriately alone with them;
Communities and beneficiaries are informed of ARDHI’s standards and how to report a concern.
10.3   Training and awareness
Safeguarding and PSEAH training is mandatory for all staff and volunteers on induction and periodically. Everyone must understand what safeguarding means, the standards expected, and how to report.
11.  Reporting a Safeguarding Concern
Anyone who witnesses, suspects or is told about a safeguarding concern has a duty to report it immediately — you do not need proof, only a genuine concern.
Report to the Designated Safeguarding Focal Point, or the Executive Director, or the Board safeguarding lead;
If the concern involves the ED or a Board member, report to the Board Chairperson or, if necessary, an external authority;
Reports may be made through the Whistleblowing Policy and are protected from retaliation.
12.  Responding to a Concern
ARDHI responds to every concern promptly, sensitively and in a survivor-centred way:
Ensure immediate safety – take urgent steps to protect anyone at risk; seek medical or police help where needed.
Receive and record – the focal point records the concern factually and confidentially (Annex A).
Support the survivor – offer or refer to appropriate support (medical, psychosocial, legal), respecting the survivor’s wishes and consent.
Assess and refer – assess the concern; refer to authorities (Police Child & Family Protection Unit, Probation & Social Welfare Officer) and to donors where required.
Investigate – conduct or commission a fair, confidential investigation; anyone implicated plays no part in it and may be suspended as a neutral precaution.
Act and learn – take disciplinary and legal action as warranted; fix any gaps that allowed the harm.
13.  Survivor Support and Referral
The survivor’s safety, wishes and dignity guide every step. ARDHI maintains referral links to medical, psychosocial, legal and protection services and supports the survivor to access them with informed consent. Support is provided without discrimination and regardless of whether the survivor wishes to pursue a formal complaint, subject to legal reporting duties for children.
14.  Confidentiality and Information Sharing
Safeguarding information is highly confidential and shared strictly on a need-to-know basis, in the survivor’s interest and per the Data Protection and Privacy Act, 2019;
The identity of survivors and reporters is protected as far as possible;
Records are stored securely with restricted access.
15.  Managing Those Accused
A person who is the subject of a safeguarding concern is treated fairly and presumed innocent pending investigation, while the survivor’s safety is protected. Precautionary measures (such as suspension or restricted contact) may be applied. Confirmed breaches are gross misconduct leading to dismissal and referral to authorities and donors.
16.  Partners, Suppliers and Visitors
ARDHI expects partners, sub-grantees, suppliers and consultants to meet equivalent safeguarding standards, and includes safeguarding clauses in agreements;
Visitors to ARDHI activities involving children or vulnerable people are briefed and supervised.
17.  Digital Safeguarding, Images and Communications
ARDHI’s tools, platforms and data are used in ways that protect people from online harm; personal data is protected under the Data Protection Policy;
Informed consent is obtained before taking or using images of beneficiaries, and especially children (with parental/guardian consent); images are respectful and never expose a person to risk;
Content that could identify or endanger a survivor is never published.
18.  Monitoring, Records and Review
The focal point maintains a confidential safeguarding register of concerns and outcomes;
Safeguarding is monitored and reported to the Board; trends inform improvements;
This policy is reviewed at least every two (2) years and after any serious incident.
