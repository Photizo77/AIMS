2. Who We Are
We are a Ugandan based NGO that drives community resilience through advocacy and systems innovations in the areas of agriculture and food security, waste management, disaster preparedness, and land governance.
2.1 Vision
A future where research informs action, and action delivers justice.
2.2 Mission
Impacting communities through research, innovations and advocacy particularly in areas of smart agriculture, waste management, disaster preparedness, health and land governance.
2.3 Core values

 | Champion policies and actions that safeguard natural resources.
 | 
Equity
 | Foster inclusive environments and advocate for fair access to resources, ensuring marginalized voices are represented and empowered.
 | 
Innovation
 | Pioneer creative solutions and leverage emerging technologies to address complex systemic challenges.
 | 
Collaboration
 | Forge purposeful partnerships to amplify collective impact and drive lasting change.
 | 3. Our Pillars
ARDHI works through three connected pillars. They are not separate departments doing separate things. Each pillar feeds the other two.

Pillar
 | What it covers
 | Typical outputs
 | 
Advocacy
 | Legal empowerment of communities, legal aid and mediation, public interest engagement, policy influencing at district and national level, coalition building.
 | Community legal empowerment programmes, resolved land disputes, policy submissions, district ordinances and byelaws, public campaigns
 | 
Research
 | Original studies on tenure security, land governance, land conflict, gender and land, and the performance of land institutions. Evaluations and assessments for others.
 | Baseline and tenure studies, policy briefs, peer reviewed and practitioner publications, evaluation reports, open data sets
 | 
Innovation
 | Designing and testing practical tools and models that make land rights easier to secure, including digital tools, community documentation models, and new approaches to dispute resolution.
 | Mobile documentation and mapping tools, community mediation models, paralegal accreditation system, decision support tools for land offices
 | 
The connection between the three is the point. Research identifies where the system fails. Innovation designs and tests a fix in a real community. Advocacy takes the tested fix and gets it adopted as policy or standard practice. A funder investing in one pillar is therefore investing in the whole chain.

5. How We Work
5.1 Our theory of change
ARDHI is not a Think Tank, but a Think and Act Tank. Established in 2024 and fully registered in 2025, we operate on the belief that if we combine advocacy to shift rules, research to generate empirical evidence, and innovation to pilot practical models across agriculture, waste, disaster preparedness, health, and land governance, communities can gain the tools, voice, and systems they need to solve persistent problems. Lasting impact requires more than just ideas, it requires pressure, proof, and prototypes working together.

If we...
 | Then...
 | Leading to...
 | 
Generate credible evidence on how land systems fail particular groups
 | Decision makers and communities can see the problem clearly and cannot dismiss it
 | Informed demand for change and a defensible advocacy position
 | 
Empower communities legally, and equip local paralegals and mediators
 | People can claim, document and defend their rights without depending on us
 | Fewer land losses, faster dispute resolution, lasting local capacity
 | 
Design and test practical innovations with communities and institutions
 | There is a working, costed model rather than a demand with no solution attached
 | Adoptable approaches that institutions can take up
 | 
Engage land institutions and policy makers with that evidence and those models
 | Rules, procedures and budgets change
 | Systemic and durable improvement in tenure security
 | 
Target women, youth, persons with disabilities and other excluded groups throughout
 | Benefits reach those who start furthest behind
 | Reduced inequality in land holding and land justice
 | 5.2 Our approach in practice
We work through existing institutions wherever they exist, rather than creating parallel structures that end when our funding ends.
We train local people rather than deploying outsiders, and we certify them so that their skills are recognised after we leave.
We publish our data and our tools, including the ones that did not work.
We deliver in the languages our participants actually speak.
We plan our exit from the day a project is designed, and we say so to the community at the first meeting.
We partner rather than compete, including with organisations that do only advocacy, because our research strengthens their case.

7. Governance and Leadership
ARDHI is governed by a Board of Directors that sets strategy, approves policy, oversees risk and safeguarding, and holds management to account. Day to day leadership sits with the Country Director, supported by the Executive Director and the senior management team. The full structure is set out in Annex 9.

 | 12. Why Fund ARDHI
Evidence first. We fund our advocacy with our own research, so our claims survive scrutiny by government and by peers.
Innovation with proof. We test approaches in real communities and publish the cost per result, so a funder knows what scale would cost.
Local delivery. Our work is carried out by Ugandan staff and by community members from the districts where we work.
Systems that pass due diligence. Safeguarding, safeguards, inclusion, MEL, risk and beneficiary selection frameworks are in place and documented in these annexes.
Inclusion that is measured. Participation quotas and disaggregated reporting are built into every project, not added at evaluation.
Designed to end well. Every project has a sustainability plan, a handover memorandum and post project follow up.
