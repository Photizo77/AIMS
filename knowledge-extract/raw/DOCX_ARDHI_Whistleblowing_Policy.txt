1.  Purpose
This Whistleblowing Policy gives everyone connected with ARDHI Law & Policy Initiative (“ARDHI”) a safe, confidential way to raise genuine concerns about wrongdoing — and a firm assurance that they will be protected for doing so. Speaking up early allows ARDHI to stop harm, correct problems, and protect the communities, funds and reputation entrusted to it.
2.  Scope
The policy applies to disclosures by any person — staff, Board members, volunteers, interns, consultants, suppliers, partners, beneficiaries or members of the public — about ARDHI’s activities, staff or associates. It complements the Anti-Fraud, Conflict of Interest and safeguarding arrangements.
3.  Legal Framework
This policy reflects the Whistleblowers Protection Act, 2010 of Uganda, which protects persons who make disclosures of impropriety in good faith, and the anti-fraud and safeguarding requirements of ARDHI’s donors.
4.  Definitions

Term
 | Meaning
 |

 |

 | An honest belief that the information is true, without malice or personal motive.
 |

 | Any harm, disadvantage or threat directed at someone for raising a concern.
 | 
Whistleblower
 | The person who makes the disclosure.
 | 5.  Principles
Safety to speak up. No one is penalised for raising a genuine concern in good faith, even if it later proves mistaken.
Confidentiality. The identity of a whistleblower is protected as far as possible.
No retaliation. Retaliation against a whistleblower is strictly prohibited and is itself a serious offence.
Fairness. Persons who are the subject of a concern are treated fairly, with the presumption of innocence.
Seriousness. Every genuine concern is taken seriously and handled promptly.
6.  What Should Be Reported
Concerns that should be raised under this policy include, but are not limited to:
Fraud, corruption, bribery, theft or misuse of ARDHI or donor funds or assets;
Abuse, exploitation, or any safeguarding concern involving children or vulnerable people (including sexual exploitation, abuse or harassment – SEAH);
Danger to the health or safety of any person;
Serious environmental harm;
Breach of the law, of donor terms, or of ARDHI policies;
Abuse of position, conflicts of interest, or nepotism; and
Deliberate concealment of any of the above.
7.  What Is Not Covered
Personal complaints about one’s own employment (pay, workload, individual grievances) are handled under ARDHI’s HR grievance procedure, not this policy — unless they involve wrongdoing of the kind listed in Section 6.

 | 9.  What Information to Provide
To help ARDHI act, a disclosure should where possible describe: what happened; who was involved; when and where; how the person came to know; and any evidence available. A whistleblower is not required to prove the concern — an honest, reasonable belief is enough.
10.  Confidentiality and Anonymity
ARDHI protects the identity of a whistleblower and shares it only where necessary to investigate or as required by law, and then only with their knowledge wherever possible.
Anonymous reports are accepted and acted on so far as the information allows, though anonymity may limit follow-up and feedback.
11.  Protection Against Retaliation
No whistleblower acting in good faith will be dismissed, demoted, harassed, victimised or otherwise disadvantaged for raising a concern.
Anyone who retaliates against a whistleblower faces disciplinary action up to dismissal, and possible legal consequences.
A whistleblower who believes they are suffering retaliation should report it immediately to the Board Chairperson.
12.  How a Concern Is Handled
Acknowledgement – the concern is acknowledged to the whistleblower (where contactable), normally within five (5) working days.
Assessment – the recipient assesses the concern and decides the appropriate route (investigation under the Anti-Fraud Policy, safeguarding process, HR, or referral).
Investigation – a fair, impartial, confidential enquiry is conducted by someone with no involvement in the matter.
Feedback – the whistleblower is told, as far as confidentiality and law allow, that the matter has been addressed and broadly how.
Action – wrongdoing that is confirmed leads to corrective, disciplinary or legal action, and control improvements.
13.  Safeguarding Disclosures
Concerns about the safety of children or vulnerable adults — including sexual exploitation, abuse or harassment — are treated with the highest priority and urgency, handled by designated safeguarding-responsible persons, and reported to authorities and donors as required. The safety of the survivor comes first at every step.
14.  Malicious or False Allegations
A concern raised in good faith that turns out to be mistaken attracts no penalty. However, allegations known to be false, or made maliciously or for personal gain, are a disciplinary matter.
15.  External Disclosure
Whistleblowers are encouraged to use internal channels first. Where the matter is not properly addressed, where internal channels are compromised, or where the wrongdoing is grave, a disclosure may be made to an appropriate external body — for example the Police, the Inspectorate of Government, a regulator, or a donor — and such disclosure, made in good faith, remains protected.
16.  Records
A confidential register of disclosures and their outcomes is maintained by the Executive Director/Board Chair and retained securely for at least seven (7) years, accessible only to authorised persons.
17.  Awareness and Training
This policy is communicated to all staff, volunteers and partners on joining and periodically, and its channels are displayed where people can see them, so that everyone knows how and where to raise a concern.
18.  Review
This policy is reviewed at least every two (2) years and after any significant case. Amendments are approved by the Board.
